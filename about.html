By: Ashkan Moghaddassi, Gage Condon, Steven Hall

Data Source: State Crime

Data Processing: We enountered very few problems regarding data processing. Our original data set was accurate and was not missing any information. Due to the format of the column names, however, we had to make some adjustments when fetching particular values. For example, to fetch the value for the total property crime rate, we used "Data\\.Rates\\.Property\\.All" for our Vega-Lite code. For the remaining visualizations, we constructed a new array with improved column names to avoid this confusion. We also added a new column, which was the sum of the property crime rates and violent crime rates. This was used in the linked area chart.

Design + Development: We decided to order our visualizations based on our natural progression of questions. To begin with, we chose to use a scatterplot to compare the property crime rates and violent crime rates for a particular year. This was the most logical way of determining if there is a correlation between these two quantitative values. We also added a brushing interaction so that violent crime rates of selected states could be more easily compared using a bar chart. Once this positive correlation was made clear, we wanted to see how the total crime rate, as well as the types of crimes committed, changed overtime for a particular state. The linked stacked area chart and area chart were the ideal choices for seeing this change overtime along with which types of crimes were most common. Finally, we wanted to compare the change in crime rates and crime distributions by analyzing the five most crime-ridden states (including D.C.). The maps clearly displayed how crime rates differed using color hue. We then used line charts to compare crime rates for the given states overtime. Finally, the side-by-side bars were ideal for comparing the specific crime types for these states. We used the median rates to account for any outliers when creating these charts. Thus, we could easily find correlations, compare crime rates, and compare crime distributions through this process.

Important Definitions:

Burglary: entry into a building illegally with intent to commit a crime, especially theft

Larceny: theft of personal property

Motor: crimes where a motor vehicle was stolen

Assault: crimes where someone made an attempt to initiate harmful or offensive contact with a person, or made a threat to do so

Murder: crimes where someone committed the unlawful killing of another human being without justification

Rape: the FBI UCR definition of rape, before 2013, is the carnal knowledge of a female forcibly and against her will

Robbery: crimes where someone took or attempted to take anything of value by force or threat of force or by putting the victim in fear
