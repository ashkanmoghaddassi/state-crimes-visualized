An Exploration of Violent and Property Crime Throughout the United States From 1960 to 2019

Project Information

Introduction

Our goal for this project is to determine how property crime rates and violent crime rates have changed for the fifty states, as well as the District of Columbia, from 1960 to 2019. We would like to determine which states have the highest property crime rates, which states have the highest violent crime rates, and if there is any correlation between property and violent crime. We also seek to highlight the breakdown for both property and violent crime for the five states with the highest crime rates. These breakdowns include burglary, larceny, and motor crime rates for property crime, as well as murder, rape, robbery, and assault for violent crime.

The Decrease in Crime Rates Since the 1990s

Lastly, we wanted to determine how crime rates have changed over time and how the distributions of property crime and violent crime compare by focusing on the top five most crime-ridden states. We selected the top five states for property crime and violent crime by taking the median of the property crime rates and violent crime rates, respectively, from 1960 to 2019. We used the median rather than the average to account for outliers in the data.
We discover that both the property crime rates and violent crime rates for these states have been on a steady decline since the 1990s. Furthermore, the distributions for property crime types are nearly identical for these states. On the other hand, the distributions for violent crime types were less uniform, with robbery and assault being the most common types of violent crime.

Conclusion

In summary, we were able to show that, in general, a positive correlation between property crime rates and violent crime rates does exist (as displayed by the first set of charts). In other words, a state with a high violent crime rate is likely to also have a high property crime rate, as expected. However, the stacked area chart proves that property crime is significantly more common than violent crime, with burglary and larceny making up a vast majority of all crimes committed. Finally, the area chart and story show that there has been a steady decline in both property crime and violent crime since the 1990s. The breakdown of property crime and violent crime for the five most crime-ridden states, displayed in the two bar charts, were also uniform among the selected states. This means that, despite having different populations, most states share similar problems in combatting crimes such as larceny, robbery, and assault.
